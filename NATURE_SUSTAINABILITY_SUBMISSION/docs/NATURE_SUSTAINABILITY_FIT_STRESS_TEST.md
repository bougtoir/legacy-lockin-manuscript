# Nature Sustainability fit stress test

Hostile editorial test — decide only: SUBMIT TO NATURE SUSTAINABILITY or
DO NOT SUBMIT — GO DIRECTLY TO GLOBAL ENVIRONMENTAL CHANGE.

## Case for desk rejection (steelman)

- The paper's strongest single-domain finding is a null; editors may see it as
  "rigorously proving nothing happens."
- No single clean causal identification (IV/RDD/natural experiment); all
  observational with frozen designs.
- Domains are heterogeneous; no pooled effect — a sceptic can call it four
  unrelated studies stapled together.
- The US transition study relies on FEMA programme records — a narrow,
  institution-specific margin.
- Nature Sustainability typically publishes positive mechanisms or novel
  sustainability solutions.

## Case for submission

- The question is squarely in scope: adaptation limits, transformational
  adaptation and lock-in are core journal topics (Kates et al. 2012; Dow et
  al. 2013; Hino et al. 2017 all published in Nature-family journals).
- The result is not a bare null: it is a **boundary identification** — four
  coordinated outcome-blind tests locate where the legacy-rigidity claim
  survives (urban margin, transition-depth signal) and where it does not.
  "Legacy is a hypothesis about constraint, not evidence of it" is an
  actionable, quotable conclusion for a large literature that currently uses
  lock-in rhetoric loosely.
- Methods novelty is real: outcome-blind locked sequential stress testing
  across domains, with single-opening immutability and transparent
  primary/secondary separation, is a methodological template the journal's
  audience can reuse.
- The cross-domain design is the point — the heterogeneity is what allows a
  *general* claim about a *general* hypothesis. GEC would frame this as a
  narrower adaptation paper.

## Risks of submission

Desk rejection risk is real (~moderate): the paper lacks a dramatic positive
mechanism. But its conclusion is strong enough to stand editorial scrutiny,
and GEC remains a viable fallback.

**DECISION: SUBMIT TO NATURE SUSTAINABILITY**
