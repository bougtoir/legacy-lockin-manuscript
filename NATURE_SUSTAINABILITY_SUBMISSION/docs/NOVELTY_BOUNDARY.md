# Novelty boundary

## What has already been shown

- Lock-in, path dependence and stranded-asset concepts are well established
  theoretically (Unruh 2000; Seto et al. 2016; David 1985; Arthur 1989;
  Page 2006) and documented in case literature (carbon/energy systems;
  individual managed-retreat cases; agricultural case studies).
- Barriers-to-adaptation frameworks exist (Moser & Ekstrom 2010; Biesbroek
  et al. 2013) and maladaptation has been defined (Barnett & O'Neill 2010;
  Magnan et al. 2016; Schipper 2020).
- Single-domain empirical estimates exist: limited yield adaptation in US
  agriculture (Burke & Emerick 2016), crop-switching potential (Rising &
  Devineni 2020; Costinot et al. 2016), urban persistence after shocks
  (Hornbeck 2012; Davis & Weinstein 2002), managed-retreat case analyses
  (Hino et al. 2017; Siders 2019; Mach et al. 2019).

## What is new here

Not that lock-in exists. Not that adaptation is difficult. Not that managed
retreat occurs. The novel contribution is that **a broad legacy-rigidity
intuition was subjected to coordinated, outcome-blind locked stress tests
across four distinct human adaptation systems and failed to generalize** —
producing a defensible boundary: legacy is a hypothesis about constraint, not
evidence of it.

## Search for equivalent multi-domain falsification

Searched terms: adaptation lock-in, infrastructure lock-in, urban lock-in,
path dependence, rigidity trap, transformational adaptation, maladaptation,
managed retreat, agricultural adaptation, crop switching, stranded assets,
sustainability transitions, climate migration, institutional lock-in,
transition barriers, exit constraints — across Nature Sustainability, Nature
Climate Change, Nature Cities, Global Environmental Change, One Earth, PNAS,
ERL, Climatic Change, World Development and adaptation/transition journals.

Finding: reviews and frameworks (e.g., development-pathway reviews such as
Gajjar et al. 2019, and recent "adaptation lock-in" conceptualizations) argue
for the importance of lock-in mechanisms but do not run cross-domain empirical
tests with frozen confirmatory designs. No equivalent multi-domain falsification
study with outcome-blind locked primary tests was found. Novelty stands, with
the honest caveat that several single-domain nulls exist (e.g., Burke &
Emerick 2016 for US agriculture) — what is new is the coordinated
cross-domain test architecture and the boundary statement, not any individual
null.
